Overview
One 18650 cell (1S, 30 A peak) feeds two independent paths: a 0.5 A logic path for the Pico
2 that has hardware undervoltage cutoff and a slide switch, and four 8 A motor paths that
only turn on when the Pico tells them to.

       Circuit A, logic power: a TPS22810 load switch feeds the Pico 2 (RP2350) and
       peripherals. It cuts off on low battery and is turned off by an SPDT slide switch.
       Circuit B, motor power: one TPS22997 load switch per ESC, high side. Their ON pins
       are driven by Pico GPIOs, so they can't turn on unless the Pico is running.
       Main effect: if the Pico loses power for any reason (switch off, undervoltage, crash),
       the pull-downs on the ON pins turn all motor power off.
       No contact in the 30 A path: the slide switch only carries the EN signal.




power architecture · logic path and four motor paths

The slide switch and the lockout control only the Pico rail. The motor switches follow the
Pico's GPIOs.

Circuit A: Pico supply (TPS22810)
The TPS22810's built-in lockout turns the Pico rail on above about 2.54 V and off around
2.41 V. The SPDT slide switch forces it into shutdown, which draws under 1 µA.

       SPDT, not SPST: EN/UVLO must never float, and the SPDT needs no pull-down that
       would drain the cell.
       Low hysteresis: after a cutoff, the unloaded cell recovers to about 2.9 V, so the rail can
       come back on. Firmware handles this (see Firmware).
       2.41 V is below the part's 2.7 V minimum. The switch still turns off, but Rds(on) rises
       near that point.
       In-spec alternative (off 2.70 V, on 3.30 V, no cycling): EN/UVLO driven by a divider
       instead of the SPDT throw: R1 156 kΩ from switched BAT+ to EN, R2 100 kΩ from EN to
       GND, R3 1.27 MΩ from VOUT to EN.


Circuit B: motor power (4 × TPS22997)
Each ESC gets its own TPS22997 on the high side, carrying about 7.5 A each at 30 A total.
Compared with the TPS22998 it adds thermal shutdown and a power-good output, in a 2 ×
1.5 mm WQFN-HR package. The ON pin takes a 3.3 V GPIO directly (VIH 0.8 V), so no gate
driver is needed.

       High side keeps the ESC grounds tied to the Pico's signal ground.
       Control: one GPIO can drive all four ON pins. Four GPIOs allow staggered turn-on
       (about 10 ms apart) to spread inrush, and shutting off one faulted ESC.
       Power good: after enabling, the Pico checks all four PG lines. A channel that never
       asserts, or drops out in flight (thermal shutdown), flags a fault, so land and disarm.
       Thermal shutdown is not a current limit. It protects the switch, but a hard short still
       loads the cell until the die heats up and trips.

Firmware and protection layers
Firmware is the main protection and the TPS22810 lockout is the backstop. The Pico cuts
motor power well before the cell reaches 2.4 V.

   1. Boot: read the cell on GPIO29 (VSYS/3, on-board divider). If it's below about 3.0 V at
       rest, keep the motors off and go into dormant mode. This also stops the reboot loop
       after a hardware cutoff.
   2. Enable motors: drive the TPS22997 ON pins high, staggered if you use separate
       GPIOs. Confirm all four PG lines go high before arming.
   3. In flight: filter the cell reading over about 0.5 s. Land at about 3.0–3.2 V resting or 2.9
       V under load, then switch the motors off.
   4. Backstop: if the firmware fails, the TPS22810 drops the Pico rail around 2.41 V. The ON
       pull-downs then shut off all motor power.


  Layer                             Trigger                             Action

  Firmware                          ~3.0–3.2 V resting / 2.9 V loaded   Motors off, dormant

  TPS22810 lockout                  ~2.41 V falling                     Pico rail off → motors off

  Slide switch                      User                                TPS22810 shutdown, under 1 µA

       Throttle protocol: the XSD7A takes DShot150/300/600, Oneshot, Multishot and PWM.
       The Pico 2 can generate DShot on its PIO blocks, one pin per ESC.
       ESC brownout: the ESC MCU runs from the cell. Hard sag near 3 V can reset it mid-
       flight. The firmware landing threshold (3.0–3.2 V resting) keeps clear of this. Each
       TPS22997 adds only about 30 mV at 7.5 A.
       Fault protection: the TPS22997 has no current limit, so a shorted ESC loads the cell
       directly. Its thermal shutdown protects the switch, and the PG line tells the Pico
       which channel dropped out.



Charger: BQ25606 + USB-C
A TI BQ25606 standalone buck charger charges the soldered 18650 from USB-C at up to 3
A, with no firmware involved. Charge current and input limit are set by resistors, so it
charges even with the Pico switched off.

                        R)

  VSET                  Float → 4.208 V charge voltage

       USB-C sink: 5.1 kΩ from each CC pin to GND. Without these, USB-C-to-C chargers
       supply no VBUS.
       Actual charge current depends on the adapter: the charger's D+/D− detection sets
       the input limit. A dedicated charger (DCP) gets 2.4 A input, an unknown adapter gets
       the 3.2 A ILIM setting, a computer port gets 500 mA. From 5 V, that's roughly 2.5–3 A
       into the cell.
       Motors stay on BAT, never on SYS. The BATFET is rated for 6 A and latches off on
       overload until VBUS is replugged.
       Power path: SYS holds at least 3.5 V while USB is connected, so the Pico runs from
       USB even with a flat cell. Unplugged, SYS sits about 50 mV above BAT, so the
       TPS22810 lockout still tracks the cell.
       Safety: this charger is the only overvoltage protection on the soldered cell. Check the
       cell datasheet allows 3 A charge; if not, raise R_ICHG.
       Programming: keep USB-C for charging only and program the Pico through its own
       USB during development. Sharing D+/D− with the charger's detection needs care.
       Thermal: about 1–1.5 W of loss at 3 A. Use the thermal pad with several vias into the
       GND plane, and keep it away from the IMU.

Source: BQ25606 datasheet


Sensors (Crazyflie set)
The sensors copy the Crazyflie 2.1 / Bolt set: a BMI088 IMU and BMP388 barometer on the
main board, plus a Flow deck v2 underneath. Staying identical means the open-source
Crazyflie drivers and state-estimator parameters carry over to the Pico.

       Flow deck v2 on the Pico: wire power, GND, the SPI lines plus the flow chip select, and
       the I2C lines from its Crazyflie deck connector. Check power and chip-select pins
       against the deck schematic before connecting.
       BMI088 start-up: the accelerometer powers up in I2C mode. One rising edge on its
       chip select switches it to SPI, and every accel read returns a dummy byte first.
       Mounting: IMU near the centre of gravity, soft-mounted. Flow deck facing straight
       down with a clear view, near the centre so rotation adds little false motion.
       Reference design: the Bolt schematic is open source and shows the BMI088 and
       BMP388 circuits.

Optional: 5.1 V rail for a Raspberry Pi Zero
A TI TPS61235P boost converter makes a fixed 5.1 V rail for a Pi Zero 2 W (or a future Pi
Zero), switched on and off by a Pico GPIO. It fully disconnects the Pi from the battery
when disabled.

       Battery cost: at 2 A output from a 3.3 V cell, the boost draws about 3.4 A. Budget it
       alongside the 30 A motor peak.
       Graceful shutdown: cutting power can corrupt the Pi's SD card. Use a handshake: the
       Pico sends a shutdown command over UART, the Pi shuts down and signals done on
       PI_STATUS (gpio-poweroff overlay), then the Pico disables the boost (with a timeout if
       PI_STATUS never comes). The Pico's low-battery logic must start this early enough.
       Pico-to-Pi link: a 4-pin JST-SH (1.0 mm, locking) on the module goes straight to the Pi:
       GND, Pico TX → Pi RX (GPIO15), Pi TX (GPIO14) → Pico RX, and PI_STATUS (Pi → Pico).
       The Pico requests shutdown with a UART command; PI_STATUS (gpio-poweroff
       overlay) tells it when the Pi is safe to cut. Only BOOST_EN crosses the main header, so
       it stays at 2 × 10. The 5.1 V power runs separately from the main board to the Pi's 5 V
       pins. Put 1 kΩ in series on each signal and keep Pico outputs high-impedance while the
       Pi is off, so neither side back-powers the other. On the Pi, use dtoverlay=disable-bt or
       miniuart-bt so GPIO14/15 get the stable PL011 UART, and disable the serial console.
       Future Pi Zero: the 3 A rating leaves headroom, but confirm its power needs when it's
       released.

Layout

       Wide pours on BAT+ and each VOUT, several vias under each TPS22997 thermal pad.
       Place each TPS22997 close to its ESC connector, with its input capacitor right at VIN.
       Check the TPS22997 land pattern before committing to hand assembly. It's a small
       fine-pitch package.

Open items

       Measure full-throttle current per motor with the 2520 props on a fresh cell; it should
       stay under the XSD7A's 7 A continuous rating.
       Add reverse-polarity protection on the ESC power leads.
       Choose between the internal 2.54 V lockout and the 2.70 / 3.30 V divider.
       Worst-case check of thresholds against datasheet min/max values.

Sources: TPS22810 datasheet · TPS22997 product page · TPS22997 datasheet
