# Pico Alvik Adapter v1.6 – production files

All files are generated by `tools/export_fab.sh` from `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.kicad_pcb`.

## 1. PCB: `Pico_Alvik_Adapter_OSHPark.zip`

RS-274X Gerbers (plain format, no X2 attributes, 4.6 coordinates in mm) with Protel file names, and one
Excellon drill file:

| File | Layer |
|---|---|
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.GTL` | Top copper |
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.GBL` | Bottom copper |
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.GTS` | Top solder mask |
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.GBS` | Bottom solder mask |
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.GTO` | Top silkscreen |
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.GBO` | Bottom silkscreen |
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.GKO` | Board outline (includes the antenna notch) |
| `Pico_Alvik_Adapter.XLN` | Drill, Excellon, inch, decimal; all holes are vias (plated) |

**OSH Park:** upload the zip at oshpark.com and choose the standard 2-layer service
(1.6 mm FR4, ENIG). OSH Park also accepts the `.kicad_pcb` file directly. The same zip works with
other fabs that take RS-274X plus Excellon.

Board parameters:

| | |
|---|---|
| Size | 24.0 × 51.0 mm (0.94 × 2.01 in), 14 × 9 mm notch at one end |
| Layers | 2, 1.6 mm |
| Min. track / space | 0.25 mm / 0.20 mm (10 / 8 mil); OSH Park min. 6 / 6 mil |
| Min. drill / annular ring | 0.30 mm / 0.15 mm (11.8 / 5.9 mil); OSH Park min. 10 / 5 mil |
| Copper to board edge | ≥ 0.40 mm (15.7 mil); OSH Park recommends 15 mil |
| Silkscreen line | ≥ 0.15 mm on text and project footprints (library footprints: 0.12 mm) |
| Vias | 25 total, tented |

KiCad DRC: 0 violations, 0 unconnected pads.

## 1b. Other fabs: same copper, fab-specific packaging

| Fab | Upload | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **OSH Park** | `Pico_Alvik_Adapter_OSHPark.zip` (or the `.kicad_pcb`) | Protel names, drill `.XLN` (inch) |
| **JLCPCB** | `Pico_Alvik_Adapter_JLCPCB.zip` | KiCad default names (Protel extensions), Excellon mm, PTH/NPTH files (NPTH is empty). PCB only, no assembly service. |
| **Seeed Fusion** | `Pico_Alvik_Adapter_Seeed.zip` | Protel names, drill `.TXT` |
| **4project.co.il** | any of the above (e.g. the OSH Park zip) | Default "6 mil / 0.3 mm" settings are enough |

Settings to choose at JLCPCB, Seeed, 4project or similar: 2 layers, 1.6 mm FR4, 1 oz copper, any mask
colour. ENIG finish recommended (flat pads for the castellated Pico and the stencil). Lead-free HASL
also works. Minimum track 6 mil, minimum hole 0.3 mm. The board doesn't need any finer options.

Silkscreen text on the bottom is 0.6–0.8 mm high, below the roughly 1 mm that JLCPCB and Seeed
recommend for crisp text. It will still print, possibly a little soft. The D1 diode symbol
doesn't rely on text size.

## 2. Stencils: `Pico_Alvik_Adapter_stencil_top.zip`, `Pico_Alvik_Adapter_stencil_bottom.zip`

Each zip holds that side's paste layer plus the board outline (`_outline.GKO`) for alignment. Both
are drawn as seen from the **top** of the board, which is the Gerber convention, so the stencil maker
mirrors the bottom one.

| Stencil | Openings |
|---|---|
| **Top** (`.GTP`) | 40 × Pico W castellation pads, **3.8 × 2.2 mm**. That is the Pico W datasheet (Fig. 6) over-paste, 0.3 mm larger per side than the 3.2 × 1.6 mm pads, so solder wets the castellations properly. |
| **Bottom** (`.GBP`) | 30 × SMD header pads (3.56 × 1.27 mm, TE 6-146132-3 recommended layout), C1 0805 (2 × 1.0 × 1.45 mm), D1 SOD-123W (2 × 1.2 × 1.2 mm) |

Solder jumpers JP2/JP3 have no paste: JP2 is bridged in copper, and JP3 stays open.

A 0.12–0.13 mm (5 mil) stainless or polyimide stencil suits both sides. TE specifies its header land
pattern for a 0.2 mm (8 mil) stencil, so with a 5 mil stencil add a little solder to the header feet by
iron, or simply solder the headers by iron (Option A). OSH Stencils
(oshstencils.com) take these files directly.

## 3. Hand assembly

Parts: U1 Pico W, J1 two TE Connectivity **6-146132-3** 1×15 2.54 mm SMD male headers (Digi-Key A124546-ND), C1 10 µF 0805, D1 Nexperia PMEG6020ER,115 (SOD-123W, Digi-Key 1727-5217-1-ND).
JP2 is already bridged in copper, and JP3 stays open.

The board has parts on **both** sides, and the headers stick about 10 mm out of the bottom (TE 6-146132-3: ~4 mm body + 5.84 mm post), so the
order matters.

**Option A – soldering iron only (simplest)**

1. **Bottom, small parts:** C1 (not polarised) and **D1**. D1's **cathode bar** goes to the end marked
   by the silkscreen bar and **VSYS**; the anode goes to the **VIN** side. Tin one pad, place, tack,
   then solder the other pad.
2. **Bottom, headers:** plug the two 1×15 headers into a breadboard or a spare female Nano socket
   strip. That holds the rows exactly 15.24 mm apart and square. Lay the board on top, bottom side
   down, and solder each SMD foot. The 6-146132-3 legs alternate sides; turn the header so the leg pattern
   matches the staggered pads (it only fits one way round). Tack the four end pins first, check alignment, then do the rest.
3. **Top, Pico W:** tin one corner pad on the board. Align the Pico so its castellations sit centred
   on the pads, with the USB at the board's USB end (silkscreen "USB" on the bottom marks the same
   end). Tack two diagonal corners, check all 40 pads line up, then solder each castellation with a
   fine tip and a little extra solder so it fills the half-hole. Support the board on its headers
   (still in the breadboard) while you work.

**Option B – hot plate / hot air for the Pico (top side first)**

1. **Top first, while the bottom is still bare:** apply paste with the **top stencil** (or a syringe),
   place the Pico W, and reflow on a hot plate with the bare bottom side down. The board is flat at
   this stage, so it sits evenly.
2. **Then the bottom by iron:** C1, D1 and the headers as in Option A, steps 1–2. Hot air works for
   C1/D1, but keep it away from the Pico on the other side.

The bottom stencil is only worth it if you reflow the bottom with hot air. With an iron, just tin
the pads.

**Checks before plugging into the Alvik**

* Continuity: no shorts between **VSYS, VIN, 3V3 and GND**. Probe the header pins: VIN = pin 30,
  3V3 = pin 17, GND = pins 4 and 29.
* D1 direction: diode test from header pin 30 (VIN, anode) to Pico pin 39 (VSYS, cathode) should read
  about 0.2–0.4 V forward, and open in reverse.
* Plug the Pico's USB in with the adapter **out** of the robot: the Pico boots, and header pin 27
  (VUSB) reads about 5 V, which is expected.
* Do the Alvik socket bring-up check from the main README ("Power design") before the first insertion.
